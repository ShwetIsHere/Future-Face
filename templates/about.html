The Motivation

Why Was Future-Face Created?

“What if an individual captured in an old CCTV image needs to be identified years later, after significant changes in their appearance?”

This question inspired me to develop Future-Face, an AI-powered project that estimates facial age and generates age-transformed facial images.

In real-world investigative scenarios, historical CCTV footage or old photographs may contain images of suspects or missing individuals. If a person remains unidentified for years, understanding how their appearance might have changed over time could potentially assist investigators in reviewing leads and comparing visual information.

Part 1: Facial Age Identification

For age identification, I utilized the UTKFace dataset, which consists of more than 24,000+ labeled images spanning children, teens, and adults across various ethnicities.

I fine-tuned a ResNet-50 deep convolutional architecture for continuous age regression and combined it with YuNet, a lightweight and ultra-fast ONNX-based face detector capable of pinpointing bounding boxes and facial landmarks even in challenging lighting conditions.

Part 2: Age Transformation & Key Challenges Solved

Challenge 1: The FG-Net Dataset & Blurry/Messy Images

Initially, I tested with the FG-Net dataset, which contains approximately 1,000 images. However, this dataset was captured decades ago; the images were low-resolution, noisy, and blurry. When training initial generative models on FG-Net, the model output messy, distorted, and inappropriate facial artifacts.

Challenge 2: The CACD Dataset & The Gender Shift Problem

To overcome the low-resolution issue, I moved to the CACD (Cross-Age Celebrity Dataset), which contains over 150,000+ high-quality images of celebrities collected across the internet.

While image resolution drastically improved, a critical new problem emerged: the model struggled with gender consistency. When given a female portrait, the model would occasionally transform her into a male subject or vice versa as age progressed.

As shown above, the input image was female, but the unconditioned latent space frequently drifted into male facial traits during advanced aging.

The Breakthrough: SAM (Style-based Age Manipulation)

To definitively solve both artifact distortion and gender drift, I integrated the SAM (Style-based Age Manipulation) model. SAM leverages StyleGAN2's disentangled latent space with a dedicated age transformation module and identity preservation loss.

By fine-tuning SAM on CACD, the generator successfully preserved core identity, gender, bone structure, and facial demeanor, producing clean, photorealistic results across the lifespan.
